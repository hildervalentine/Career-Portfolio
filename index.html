🏆 Breakthrough Project

Tenure Regularization: Turkana County, Kenya

Some projects teach you skills. Others define your purpose. This project marked a defining moment in my journey as an urban planner.

During my time working on data collection for the implementation of a county development plan in Turkana County, I engaged with numerous project-affected persons. While each interaction deepened my understanding of development on the ground, one encounter fundamentally reshaped how I view my role in this profession.

I met a woman who, despite having only one eye, was actively involved in sensitising her community about the tenure regularisation process. Her commitment was not symbolic but was practical, informed, and deeply rooted in care for her people. She took the time to walk with me in the settlements and took me along the river, pointing out areas of silt accumulation and explaining how these changes were affecting livelihoods and land use.

In that moment, she was not a "beneficiary" of development; she was a steward of knowledge, resilience, and place. This revealed to me that the true purpose of planning goes beyond drawings, policies, and reports.

Marginalised communities are not passive recipients of development, nor problems to be solved, but essential contributors to our shared social and environmental fabric. This experience reaffirmed why I chose this career path: to plan with people, not for them, and to ensure that development is inclusive, dignified, and grounded in lived realities.

Sustainable Development

Imagine a world where communities are shaped by inclusivity, adaptability, and sustainability — where people can live dignified lives regardless of age, income, or social class.

In this world, development is not measured by the height of skyscrapers rising beside informal settlements, but by how well cities function for everyone. Cities are designed to be walkable for children, accessible for the elderly, safe for women, and supportive of livelihoods for all.

Sustainable urban development must reduce inequality, promote social cohesion, and balance environmental, economic, and social needs. True sustainability is not just about green buildings or modern infrastructure — it is about creating urban systems that are resilient, just, and capable of supporting diverse communities both now and in the future.
